# Source register

Review date: **10 November 2026**.

This register separates two kinds of statements.

- **Verified on this review** means a person on this project opened the official page or PDF named here and the fact below was visible in that file or page. “Visible” includes Army Publishing Directorate record fields and passages in the publication text.
- **Still unresolved** means the fact is missing, conflicting, behind a login, taken from a news story or a secondary index, or only partly readable. Unresolved items are not design rules.

A status of ACTIVE on a catalog page is a fact about that page on the day it was opened. It is not a standing certificate that the publication remains current. Before implementation, and again before any later citation, re-open the Army Publishing Directorate or the DoD issuances site. An older index, including a mirrored “active” flag on a non-official site, is not evidence of currency. Secondary sites are listed only where they explain a dead end. They are not authorities.

Official homes:

- Army publications: [armypubs.army.mil](https://armypubs.army.mil/)
- DoD issuances: [esd.whs.mil/DD](https://www.esd.whs.mil/DD/)
- Code of Federal Regulations: [ecfr.gov](https://www.ecfr.gov/)
- United States Code: [uscode.house.gov](https://uscode.house.gov/)

## How to read an entry

Each entry has the authority layer, what this review verified, what remains open, and the direct link that was used. Paragraph, chapter, and table references are included only where that part of the publication was actually seen.

## Army Directive 2024-09

- **Layer:** Army directive (Secretary of the Army policy).
- **Title:** Army Safety and Occupational Health Management System.
- **Direct links:** [PDF](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN41915-ARMY_DIR_2024-09-000-WEB-1.pdf) and [APD record](https://armypubs.army.mil/productmaps/pubform/Details.aspx?PUB_ID=1029703).

**Verified on this review**

- APD record fields seen: number ARMY DIR 2024-09, date 08/07/2024, title as above, proponent ASA (IE&E), status ACTIVE, associated publication AR 385-10.
- The PDF is a memorandum. Paragraph 1 cites 29 CFR 1960, DoDI 6055.01 (14 October 2014, incorporating Change 3, effective 21 April 2021), AR 5-1, and AR 385-10 (24 July 2023).
- Paragraph 2 directs implementation of ASOHMS.
- Paragraph 3 applies the directive to the Regular Army, the Army National Guard / Army National Guard of the United States, and the U.S. Army Reserve.
- Paragraph 4 says DoDI 6055.01 requires an integrated system rather than compliance-based individual programs.
- Paragraph 5 names ASOHMS as the Army standard and says organizations may use more stringent industry standards where mission needs require them. Paragraph 5b lists four characteristics of an effective system: risk identification and management, two-way communication, use of data and trends, and treating safety and occupational health as part of decisions before loss occurs.
- Paragraph 7 says ASA (IE&E) will publish further implementing guidance within one month, and that commands and organizations should be fully compliant by the end of calendar year 2030.
- Paragraph 8 says ASA (IE&E) has oversight and will incorporate the directive into AR 385-10 within two years of the directive.
- Paragraph 9 says the directive is rescinded on publication of the revised regulation.

**Still unresolved**

- Whether the implementing guidance required by paragraph 7 was published.
- Whether AR 385-10 was revised after 7 August 2026. On this review the APD record for AR 385-10 still showed 24 July 2023, and the directive record still showed ACTIVE. That pair of catalog facts does not prove a revision was skipped. It means this review did not find a superseding edition.
- The directive text reviewed does not itself list the 48 criteria or the three maturity stages. Those phrases come from other public Army pages, recorded below.

## AR 385-10

- **Layer:** Army regulation.
- **Title:** The Army Safety and Occupational Health Program.
- **Direct links:** [PDF](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN34981-AR_385-10-000-WEB-1.pdf) and [APD record](https://armypubs.army.mil/productmaps/pubform/Details.aspx?PUB_ID=1024611).

**Verified on this review**

- APD record fields seen: date 07/24/2023, title as above, proponent ASA (IE&E), status ACTIVE, associated items including PAM 385-10 and ARMY DIR 2024-09.
- The PDF states it is effective 24 August 2023 and supersedes AR 385-10 dated 24 February 2017. The summary of change says the title changed from “The Army Safety Program” and that paragraph 1-9 incorporates ASOHMS.
- Paragraph 1-9 names six capability objectives: (a) leadership engagement and personnel/Soldier participation (program management); (b) training and promotion; (c) inspections/assessments; (d) mishap, incident, and illness reporting and investigation; (e) hazard analysis and countermeasures; (f) health protection and readiness. It says mandatory ASOHMS procedures are in DA Pam 385-10.
- Paragraph 1-11 says the more stringent requirement applies when this regulation conflicts with a statutory or regulatory requirement.
- Paragraph 1-17 describes the Safety and Occupational Health Advisory Council and lists examples of metric data, including ASOHMS implementation status, mishap summaries, and hazard-abatement status for facility hazards associated with risk assessment code (RAC) 1, 2, or 3. Minutes are retained at least five years.
- Paragraph 6-2 is the regulation’s risk-management pointer. The summary of change says paragraph 6-2 integrates the process from DA Pam 385-30.
- Paragraph 6-5 states the hierarchy of controls for industrial-based operations: elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls, personal protective equipment.
- Chapter 7, paragraph 7-1, describes occupational health as prevention programs and services, including medical surveillance and fitness-for-duty work performed by health professionals. Paragraph 7-2 points to AR 40-5 and DA Pam 40-11. SENTINEL uses this chapter as the line between program information and individual medical information.

**Still unresolved**

- The full mishap-classification and reporting rules inside this regulation were not transcribed. Use DA Pam 385-40 for procedures after that pamphlet is read for the specific threshold.
- Whether a revision incorporating Army Directive 2024-09 has been published since the APD page was opened. See the directive entry.
- The regulation’s paragraph 1-9 order and wording are not identical to later public articles. That conflict is documented in the [ASOHMS mapping](03-asohms-mapping.md). It is not resolved by picking one label and hiding the other.

## ATP 5-19

- **Layer:** Doctrine (Army techniques publication).
- **Title:** Risk Management.
- **Direct links:** [PDF](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN34181-ATP_5-19-000-WEB-1.pdf) and [APD record](https://armypubs.army.mil/ProductMaps/PubForm/Details.aspx?PUB_ID=1024101).

**Verified on this review**

- APD record fields seen: date 11/09/2021, title RISK MANAGEMENT, proponent T2COM, status ACTIVE, prescribed form DD FORM 2977.
- The PDF says it supersedes ATP 5-19 dated 14 April 2014 and is approved for public release.
- Paragraph 1-14 states the five steps: identify the hazards; assess the hazards; develop controls and make risk decisions; implement controls; supervise and evaluate.
- Paragraphs 1-7 and 1-8 describe a risk decision as the determination to accept or not accept risk, made at the appropriate level, with risk information passed up and risk tolerance passed down.
- Paragraphs 1-33 through 1-38 define probability: frequent (A), likely (B), occasional (C), seldom (D), unlikely (E). The “about 500 exposures” and “about 1,000 exposures” sentences are examples inside paragraphs 1-34 and 1-35. They are not a formula that software should calculate by itself.
- Paragraphs 1-39 through 1-43 and Table 1-2 define severity: catastrophic (I), critical (II), moderate (III), negligible (IV).
- Paragraphs 1-44 through 1-48 and Table 1-1 define the four risk levels. The combinations stated in the paragraphs are:
  - Extremely high: IA, IB, IIA.
  - High: IC, ID, IIB, IIC, IIIA.
  - Medium: IE, IID, IIIB, IIIC, IVA.
  - Low: IIE, IIID, IIIE, IVB, IVC, IVD, IVE.
- Paragraph 1-44 says the step 2 level is an initial assessment and is revised when step 3 is complete.
- Paragraph 1-49 says practitioners reassess and determine residual risk after controls, and the responsible commander accepts or does not accept residual risk.
- Paragraph 1-50 lists control categories: educational, physical, and hazard elimination.
- Paragraph 1-59 says DD Form 2977 must document at least the what, who, and how of controls.
- Paragraph 1-74 says organizations should use DD Form 2977 for deliberate assessments.

**Still unresolved**

- The drawn Table 1-1 was cited by the paragraphs. The cell list above is taken from paragraphs 1-45 through 1-48, which were read. A future implementer should still place the paragraph text next to the lookup table in code review so a transcription error is visible.
- Real-time risk management is described in the same publication and was not designed here. The first milestone is a deliberate, recorded assessment.

## DA Pam 385-30

- **Layer:** DA pamphlet (procedures).
- **Title:** Risk Management.
- **Direct links:** [PDF](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/pdf/web/p385_30.pdf) and [APD record](https://armypubs.army.mil/ProductMaps/PubForm/Details.aspx?PUB_ID=83961).

**Verified on this review**

- APD record fields seen: date 12/02/2014, title RISK MANAGEMENT, proponent DAS, status ACTIVE, prescribed form DA FORM 7632.
- The PDF is the 2 December 2014 pamphlet and says it supersedes DA Pam 385-30 dated 10 October 2007. The summary of change says it introduces DD Form 2977, rescinds DA Form 7566, and updates the severity and risk-acceptance authority table.
- Paragraph 1-1 says the pamphlet carries out AR 385-10 and points to ATP 5-19 for techniques in the operational environment. The ATP it could reflect in 2014 is not the 9 November 2021 revision.
- Table 3-4, as read in the text extract, associates risk symbols with risk assessment codes: EH with 1 (extremely high), H with 2 (high), M with 3 (medium), and L with 4 (low). The low row’s formatting in the extract was broken, but the tokens “L” and “4 Low” were adjacent. Treat RAC 4 = low as read, and re-check the printed table before coding the crosswalk.
- Paragraphs 4-4 and 4-5 discuss making risk decisions and documenting them. Paragraph 4-4c says Army headquarters commanders establish and publish approval authority or adopt Table 4-1 in writing.
- Paragraph 4-4 says the appropriate authority depends on duration, level of risk, and ownership of the resources needed to control the hazard. Duration is not split into shorter pieces to reach a lower approval level. Recurring missions use the full anticipated period, not one occurrence.
- A passage in the same chapter states that the overall residual level for an operation is equal to or higher than the highest single-hazard residual, and that a commander may judge the overall level higher when several hazards combine. That passage was read. It is about the overall mission level. It is not permission to change one hazard’s matrix cell, and it is not permission to set the overall level lower than the highest hazard.
- Paragraph 4-5 distinguishes a deviation from a written standard and a general risk acceptance. Both, as read, point documentation to Table 4-1.
- Table 4-1’s readable portions: the table is titled as risk acceptance authority for safety-standards deviation. Extremely high risk for one month or less is associated with a general officer. Note 3 says high risk beyond one month, or extremely high risk, is always accepted by a general officer or flag officer. High risk for one month or less is associated with a brigade commanding officer or responsible O-6. Paragraph 4-4f(2) says that when the public is exposed to high or extremely high risk, the risk-acceptance authority is the Army headquarters commanding general. Note 4 points fielded acquisition programs to DA Pam 385-16. Note 5 points range deviations to AR 385-63.
- Table 4-2 is identified as military-to-civilian grade equivalents. The grade rows were not transcribed.

**Still unresolved**

- This pamphlet remains on the APD catalog and predates ATP 5-19 (2021). Currency as a catalog status was verified. Fitness of every 2014 procedure against the 2021 ATP was not.
- Table 4-1 column headers and the medium and low rows were misaligned in the text extract. They are not verified and will not be implemented.
- Note 1, written delegation to the next lower command level, was visible as a note. Which cells it applies to was not reliable. Delegation will not be implemented.
- The overall-mission “equal or higher” judgment will not be built in the first milestone. It needs a clean re-read of the printed paragraphs before any later mission-level worksheet uses it.
- DA Form 7632 is for deviations involving ammunition and explosives or chemical agents (paragraph 4-6). That path is out of the first milestone. No override screen will stand in for it.

## Subject-matter-expert review

Ask a safety and occupational health specialist to review, before any of these become coded rules:

- A controlled extract of the 48 criteria and the official maturity rubric.
- DA Pam 385-30 Table 4-1 in the printed PDF, especially medium, low, and delegation.
- DA Pam 385-40 mishap classes and timelines.
- Whether any AR 385-10 revision after 24 July 2023 now exists.
- The current WHS file for DoDI 6055.01, DoDI 6055.05, and DoDI 6055.07.
- AR 11-35, once the PDF is opened, and only if occupational-health risk rules are actually going to be built.
